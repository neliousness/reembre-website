## The ADHD leaving the house checklist (copy this)

The checklist below has three layers on purpose. Mixing "things to carry" with "things to check" is how one long list turns into wallpaper: you skim it, see familiar words, and miss the one line that mattered today.

**Every trip** (lives on the launchpad, rarely changes):

1. Keys
2. Phone
3. Wallet or card
4. Medication you carry during the day
5. One personal item you forget often (badge, glasses, earbuds, inhaler)

**Today only** (written the night before, deleted after):

1. The one-off item: a parcel to return, a form to sign, a gym bag
2. Anything that lives in the fridge, like lunch
3. Anything you promised someone you would bring

**House checks** (done at the door, before you lock it):

1. Stove and oven off
2. Door locked behind you
3. Windows shut if it might rain

Copy it into whatever you will actually look at: a card taped inside the front door, a note on your phone, or the [free checklist builder](/checklist). If a layer has more than five lines, cut it. A long list gets read like a paragraph, and that is the failure you are trying to fix.

## Why do people with ADHD forget things when leaving the house?

People with ADHD forget things when leaving the house because grabbing an item on the way out is a prospective memory task: remembering to do something later, without a prompt. A December 2025 [CHADD Attention Magazine article](https://chadd.org/attention-article/remembering-the-future-how-adhd-affects-prospective-memory-and-how-to-work-with-it/) by memory researcher Daniella Karidi explains that ADHD makes both kinds of prospective memory harder, and that time-based reminders ("leave at 8:10") are usually harder than event-based ones ("when I reach the door").

That distinction is the whole design of the checklist above. Each layer is tied to an event you will definitely hit: loading the launchpad, writing tomorrow's list at night, touching the door handle. None of it relies on remembering at the right minute. We go deeper into the transition itself in [why you forget things when you leave the house](/blog/why-do-you-forget-things-when-you-leave-the-house). The short version is that this is a pattern in how memory works, not a character flaw.

## How do you make a leaving checklist actually stick?

A leaving checklist sticks when it is attached to a place and a habit you already have. The four habits below come up again and again in ADHD advice, and each one covers a different layer of the list.

### Build a launchpad by the door

A launchpad is one fixed spot by the exit where every-trip items live: a basket, a bowl, a hook with a shelf above it. ADHD coach Sandy Maynard describes hers in [ADDitude's expert organization tips](https://additudemag.com/slideshows/organization-hacks-top-adhd-experts/) as a basket by the front door with her keys, purse and phone charger. The point is that you only have to remember one place instead of five objects. If you can, put it next to an outlet so the phone charges there overnight.

### Write the "today only" layer the night before

The night before is when you still remember the parcel, because you just saw the email about it. Put the item itself on the launchpad if it fits, or put a note on the door handle if it lives in the fridge. ADDitude's same tip list includes laying out clothes and critical items the previous evening.

### Do a three-point check

[Psych Central](https://psychcentral.com/blog/adhd-tip-how-to-stop-losing-your-stuff/) recommends checking for your essentials three times: while gathering things, once everything is gathered, and at the door. The third check matters most, because that is where the "one last email" or bathroom trip makes you put your keys down somewhere new. Saying the items out loud (keys, phone, wallet) while you pat your pockets turns it into something your hands do even when your head is elsewhere.

### Keep each list short and specific to the trip

A work morning and a gym evening need different lists. One generic list for every trip gets ignored, because half of it never applies. Our [guide to not forgetting things when you leave](/blog/how-to-stop-forgetting-things-when-you-leave-the-house) covers why lists turn into wallpaper and how to keep them fresh.

## What should a phone reminder cover, and what should it not?

A phone reminder that runs when you leave should cover the things you can still go back for, and nothing you have to check at the door. That split comes from how leaving reminders actually work, and it is the part most ADHD checklists get wrong.

Phones notice you have left a place with geofencing, a boundary drawn around the place that the operating system watches. A leaving prompt cannot fire the second you step outside, because GPS drifts by tens of meters, especially indoors. A reminder that fired on the first wobble would buzz while you were still in the kitchen. So Reembr waits until you have moved some real distance from the place before it shows your list. We cover the mechanics in [what a geofence reminder is](/blog/what-is-a-geofence-reminder).

That delay decides what belongs on the phone:

| Item | Phone prompt after you leave | Habit at the door |
|---|---|---|
| Keys, wallet, badge, meds | Good: you can walk back a block | Also good: the three-point check |
| Today's one-off item | Good: this is what it catches best | Note on the door handle |
| Stove, iron, door lock | Too late to be useful as the main check | Required |

The practical rule: put the every-trip and today-only layers on the phone as a backup, and keep house checks as a door habit. If you worry about the stove after you have left, a door photo or a smart plug answers that question better than any reminder.

## How do you reload the launchpad when you get home?

You reload the launchpad by giving arriving home its own short checklist. Most launchpad advice stops at "keep a spot by the door" and skips the part where everything has to land back there in the evening, which is exactly when attention is lowest.

An arrival list can be three lines: keys and wallet on the launchpad, phone on the charger, tomorrow's one-off item next to them. In Reembr every checklist has a Runs when setting of Leave, Arrive or Both, so the same saved place can hold a leaving list and an arrival list. ## Does an ADHD leaving checklist work for kids?

The same three layers work for kids, with the wording and the checks done together. Most printable ADHD checklists online are written for school mornings, and they lean on pictures and an adult checking in. For an adult, the list has to work when nobody is there to ask "have you got your keys?", which is why the launchpad, the door habit and a backup prompt matter more than how the list looks.

If you want a deeper comparison of the methods people try, from sticky notes to tracker tags, see [five ways to remember your keys, wallet and charger](/blog/ways-to-remember-keys-wallet-charger-before-you-leave). None of this replaces support from a clinician if forgetting is affecting your work or health; it is a system for the door, not a treatment.
