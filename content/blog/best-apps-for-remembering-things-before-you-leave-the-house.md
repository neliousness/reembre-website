Full disclosure up front: we make Reembr, and it is in this list. We have tried to describe every app the way we would want ours described, from its current store listing, and to say plainly where another app is the better pick.

## Which apps remind you to take things when you leave?

Six apps are built specifically to show a checklist when you head out, and two built-in reminder apps can do a simpler version for free. The table is based on each app's store or launch listing on 2026-10-20; prices and features change, so check the listing before you pay.

| App | Phones | What starts the checklist | Price (as listed) | Developer's privacy label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reembr | iPhone, Android | Leaving or arriving at a saved place (OS geofence) | Free | Places and movements stay on the phone |
| Before You Go: Checklists | iPhone, Apple Watch | Starting to leave, if items are unchecked | Free, Pro upgrade | Data Not Collected |
| ReadyKin (formerly Before Leaving) | iPhone, iPad, Mac, Watch | Location-based checklists, method not stated | Free, subscription or lifetime premium | Identifiers and usage data, some used for tracking |
| Paranoid List | iPhone | Lists organized by place; you open and tick them | Free, one-time unlock | Data Not Collected |
| Checking Rituals | iPhone | Leaving and returning to places you choose | Free | Data Not Collected |
| LeftBehind | Android | Trusted Wi-Fi, movement state, or manual status | Free and Pro tiers, no prices listed | No account, no server (maker's claim) |
| Apple Reminders | iPhone | A single reminder when you leave or arrive | Free, built in | Apple |
| Samsung Reminder | Galaxy phones | A single reminder when you leave or arrive | Free, built in | Samsung |

Two things stand out. Almost every dedicated leaving-checklist app is iPhone-only, and most have very few App Store ratings, so treat any of them, ours included, as something to try for a week rather than a proven pick.

## How does each app notice that you are leaving?

A leaving-checklist app notices you have left in one of three ways: a GPS geofence, a change in Wi-Fi or motion, or not at all, because you open the list yourself. The method matters more than the feature list, because it decides when the reminder shows up and how often it misses.

### Location: Reembr, Checking Rituals, Before You Go, Apple Reminders, Samsung Reminder

Most location-based apps use a geofence. You draw a boundary around a place and the phone's operating system tells the app when you cross it. The OS does this with its own location services, so the app does not need to run GPS all day.

The catch, which we see daily while building Reembr, is that a leave event waits until you are clearly outside the boundary. GPS jitters by tens of meters indoors, so firing at the first wobble would mean false alarms while you are still in the kitchen. Expect the reminder a short walk or drive from the door, not at it. We explain the mechanics in [what a geofence reminder is](/blog/what-is-a-geofence-reminder).

### Wi-Fi and motion: LeftBehind

LeftBehind watches for your phone dropping off a trusted network or starting to move. It does not follow your GPS position, which is a real privacy plus, although Android still asks for location permission before any app can read the name of the Wi-Fi network you are on. It depends on where your Wi-Fi signal ends, and it cannot tell your home network going down from you walking out.

### Manual: Paranoid List and any checklist app

You open the list before you go. This never fires late and never fires wrongly, but it only works on the days you remember to open it, which are rarely the days you forget your keys.

## The apps in more detail

### Reembr

Reembr shows a checklist after you leave a saved place, so you can turn back while it is still easy. You save places on a map, as a circle or a perimeter you draw, attach one or more checklists, and set each place to run when you leave, arrive, or both. Detection uses the phone's built-in geofencing on iOS and Android, and saved places plus every arrival and departure stay on the phone.

Its honest tradeoff is timing: the checklist appears after you have moved a real distance from the place, not at the door. On Android it needs Android 10 or newer, and some Samsung phones need battery optimization turned off for the app before reminders fire reliably.

**Best for:** a recurring checklist that runs automatically on both iPhone and Android, with location data kept off any server.

### Before You Go: Checklists

Before You Go is an iPhone and Apple Watch checklist app that sends a notification when you start to leave and important items are still unchecked. Checklists sync between iPhone and Watch through iCloud, and its listing shows a paid Pro upgrade on top of the free app.

**Best for:** Apple Watch owners who want to tick items off on the wrist.

### ReadyKin (formerly Before Leaving)

ReadyKin is the app that used to be called Before Leaving: its listing says so in the release notes, and the old App Store link now opens ReadyKin. It has grown into a family organizer with location-based checklists, shared lists, traffic-aware timing and a premium subscription. Its privacy label lists device identifiers and usage data, some of it used to track you for third-party advertising.

**Best for:** families who want shared lists and planning features and are comfortable with an ad-supported model.

### Paranoid List

Paranoid List organizes checklists by place, such as Home, Car, Work and the Airport, shows a "Ready to Go" banner when a list is complete, and offers temporary Travel Packs for trips. Its listing does not say it alerts you when you leave, so treat it as a well-organized list you open yourself.

**Best for:** people who already have the habit of checking a list at the door and want a tidy one.

### Checking Rituals

Checking Rituals is a free iPhone app for the "did I turn off the stove?" kind of checking. Items can require a photo, so you can look at the picture later instead of driving back, and it uses location only to notice when you leave and return to places you pick.

**Best for:** checking things in the house (stove, locks, windows) rather than things you carry.

### LeftBehind

LeftBehind is an Android checklist app for home, work, hotels and travel stops that suggests your list from trusted Wi-Fi and movement instead of GPS. Its maker says it has no accounts and no server.

**Best for:** Android users who would rather the app never use GPS.

## Can Apple Reminders or Samsung Reminder do this for free?

Yes, for a single reminder. Apple Reminders on iPhone and Samsung Reminder on Galaxy phones can both fire a reminder when you leave or arrive at a place, with nothing to install. Google Keep used to as well, but [Google removed Keep's location reminders](/blog/google-keep-location-reminders) when its reminders moved into Google Tasks in late 2025. Step-by-step instructions for both are in our guide to [setting a location-based reminder on iPhone and Android](/blog/how-to-set-a-location-reminder-iphone-android).

The limit is that each one is a single reminder, not a list that comes back every time you leave. That is the gap the dedicated apps above are built to fill.

## What do the privacy labels actually tell you?

An App Store privacy label is what the developer says about itself. Each label carries Apple's own note that "this information has not been verified by Apple", and a 2022 study of App Store apps found at least 276 that sent data their label did not declare ([Lalaine study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06274)). A label that says Data Not Collected is a promise, not an audit.

For a leaving-checklist app the question that matters is narrower: does the list of places you visit, and when you come and go, leave your phone? A geofence app can work without ever uploading that, as we explain in [does a geofence app track your location](/blog/does-a-geofence-app-track-your-location). Look for an app that says so specifically about location, not one that only says it is "private".

## Which one should you use?

Pick by what starts the checklist and which phone you have.

1. **You want it automatic on both iPhone and Android:** Reembr.
2. **You live on an Apple Watch:** Before You Go.
3. **Your worry is the stove or the lock, not your keys:** Checking Rituals.
4. **You are on Android and want detection without GPS:** LeftBehind.
5. **You need one reminder, once:** the Reminders app already on your phone.
